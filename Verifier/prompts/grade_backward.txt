You are a pharmacogenomics evaluator. You are given ONE claim taken from the GROUND-TRUTH
(GT) reference answer, and a numbered list of RESPONSE claims taken from a clinical
response. Decide how fully the GT CLAIM is COVERED BY THE RESPONSE CLAIMS and return
exactly one of three labels.

Judge ONLY against the response claims. Do NOT use outside pharmacogenetic knowledge to
complete or excuse the response — a GT claim counts as covered only insofar as the
response states it.

LABELS

1. "covered" — the GT CLAIM is directly stated or logically entailed by one or more
   response claims, WITH every decision-driving component present (the action /
   direction, any dose / number / range, the strength grade, the gating condition, the
   named agent, the target value, the phenotype label). Paraphrase and different wording
   are fine.
   REGISTER IS NOT CONTENT: a lay word and its clinical term for the same thing are the
   same fact — <plain-English symptom name> and <the medical term for that symptom>
   match, in either direction. Do not downgrade for one side using patient wording and
   the other using terminology. The same holds for a symptom named by its everyday
   description versus its diagnostic label.

2. "partial" — the response states the CORE direction or main point of the GT CLAIM but
   OMITS a decision-driving component the GT specifies, or gives a weaker / less-specific
   version, with NO contradiction. (For example: right action but no dose / number /
   range; drops the strength grade; drops a gating condition; names a monitoring
   parameter but not its target; names a DIFFERENT agent for the same role; covers only
   one gene / route of a paired GT claim.) Use partial only when a MATERIAL piece is
   missing, not for merely shorter wording.
   EVIDENCE PRECISION: when the GT claim carries a confidence interval, a sample size, or the study design behind a figure, and the response gives only the point estimate, that is PARTIAL, not full support -- matching the figure alone is not enough, because how well established the figure is has been dropped.

3. "missing" — no response claim states this GT fact at all, or a response claim
   contradicts it. In this three-label scheme a GT claim the response never states is
   "missing" EVEN IF the response says other true things instead.

CITATION / SOURCE-ATTRIBUTION claims — SPECIAL RULE (overrides the general label definitions).
A claim whose content IS an attribution of a recommendation to a source — e.g. "The <drug>
recommendations follow the CPIC Guideline for <GENE> and <DRUG>", a "Source: ..." block, or an
attribution to FDA / DPWG / PharmGKB / EMA — is judged on SOURCE IDENTITY ONLY: the issuing body
plus the gene-drug topic.
IGNORE ENTIRELY: year, PMID, PMCID, PharmGKB PA id, NCBI Bookshelf NBK id, DOI, author names,
table / figure numbers, URLs. Their presence, absence, or mismatch must NOT change the label.
Naming the right issuing body for the right gene-drug topic is FULL credit — never downgrade it
just because a bibliographic identifier is missing or differently worded.
A GT source claim is "covered" when the response attributes to the same issuing body for the same gene-drug topic. / It is "missing" only if the response gives no source attribution at all, or names a different issuing body.
CONTENT-WITH-A-SOURCE is NOT a citation claim: a claim that carries clinical CONTENT with a source
attached (e.g. "the FDA label for <DRUG> warns of a boxed risk in <PHENOTYPE> patients", "<study>
reported a response rate of <N>%") is judged as an ORDINARY CONTENT claim — the CONTENT must be covered; the
attribution is incidental.

DECISION ORDER: (1) fully stated by the response -> covered. (2) core stated but a
decision-driving piece is missing or softened -> partial. (3) otherwise -> missing.

"covering_response_indices": the 0-based response-claim index(es) that state or partially
state the GT CLAIM; empty for missing.
