RULES
1. One fact per claim. The subject of a claim is the subject the source gives that fact: the phenotype, drug, population or artifact named in the sentence that states it, or the antecedent the source gives its pronoun. Copy that subject. Where the source states a fact with no phenotype, the claim carries no phenotype; where it names no drug, the claim names no drug; a fact the source states for one drug is not restated for another. The question's genotype and drug, a noun from a neighbouring sentence, and the value of an artifact's field are not the subject of a fact the source does not attach them to; a lookup's "general population" is its population field, and a population field, row label or caption is an artifact fact of its own: "The <artifact>'s population field reads <value> for <drug>." Replace a pronoun with its antecedent in the source ("these drugs" becomes the drugs the source's sentence names); where the source gives none, write the fact in the source's own terms without one. A template slot for <GENE> <Phenotype> or <drug> is filled from the source's sentence or its block header; where neither gives one, the slot is omitted. Add age, weight, indication, prior exposure, weeks on therapy, dose already given or what the test interrogated where the source states them for that fact. When the source rests the fact on two genes, restate both.
2. Copy numbers, doses, ranges, percentages, grades and cited sources verbatim. Every figure the source prints appears in some claim: dose, %, range, fold-change, AUC or Cmax change, OR/HR/RR with CI, incidence, residual activity, count, interval. Where the claim being written has no place for a figure, the figure takes its own claim, attached to the subject it describes. A risk's magnitude stays inside the risk claim.
3. Emit only what the answer states. Do not restate the genotype alone. Use the question only to attach the genotype, drug and case attributes to a fact.
4. Mechanism. Emit a mechanism claim where the answer states the relation. The verb follows the source's verb and the gene's role: metabolizing enzyme, "<Drug> is metabolized by <enzyme>"; prodrug, "<Drug> is a prodrug activated by <enzyme> to <metabolite>"; transporter, "<Drug> is transported by <transporter>"; inhibition, "<Drug> inhibits <enzyme>"; target, "<Gene> is the target of <drug>". Name the gene against the species it acts on. A gene that clears the active metabolite is stated against that metabolite; the prodrug's activation by a non-pharmacogene process is stated as "<prodrug> is a prodrug converted by <process> to <active species>".
5. Evidence grade. Copy the grade inside the claim it grades, verbatim ("... High."). Do not write it as "(CPIC strength: <X>)".
6. Citation. One claim per issuing source, in the form "The <topic> recommendations follow <source verbatim>", carrying every identifier: guideline name, table or figure, URL, PMCID, PharmGKB id, NBK id, author and year. A sentence with clinical content and a source name is a content claim. A pointer to the pipeline's own input sections ("RETRIEVED EVIDENCE", "STRUCTURED INPUT", "per Table N" with no publication, "(per <SECTION>)") is not a citation; strip it from every claim. A statement about how an artifact is built (title, key, population field, caption, which artifact carries what) is an Artifact structure claim.
7. Split.
   a. A block header ("For <indication>:", "In patients with <condition>:") is carried by every claim drawn from the block.
   b. A mechanism claim states the gene-drug relation only; the downstream effect or consequence clause ("..., so ...", "..., making it ...") is its own claim.
   c. A comparison is its own claim.
   d. Do not reword one fact into several claims. Do not merge two facts into one claim.
8. Ignore <think> blocks and "Step-by-Step Reasoning" or "FINAL ANSWER" scaffolding; decompose the substantive text. A concluding line that applies a clause to the case is a fact; emit it.
9. For a question with no gene, decompose each clinical fact the answer states, one per claim; the phenotype and grade templates do not apply.
