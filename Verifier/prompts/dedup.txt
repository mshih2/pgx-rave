You deduplicate a list of atomic claims decomposed from one pharmacogenomic answer by two independent streams. Keep every distinct fact once. A survivor is copied verbatim from the input; nothing is rewritten, paraphrased or merged into new wording.

SAME ASSERTION. Two claims are one fact when they have the same subject and say the same thing about it: the same directive, outcome, relation or value, in the same direction, for the same drug, phenotype or population, artifact or issuing body, and scope. Wording, voice, word order, an abbreviation (PM, Poor Metabolizer) and a synonym (avoid, not recommended, recommends against) do not change an assertion. A claim that mentions the content of another claim while asserting something else about it (what a table grades, what a label prints, what follows from a mechanism, what the case does with a clause) makes a different assertion: keep both, emit no group.

SURVIVOR. Among claims making the same assertion, the survivor is the member that carries everything the others carry: a condition, a qualifier, a source name, a strength label, a number, or a wider drug name ("(es)florabine" covers "esflorabine"). Where no member carries everything the others carry, keep them all and emit no group. Between members carrying the same content, the one with more citation identifiers (DOI, PMID, PMCID) survives, then the lower index. Position does not choose the survivor.

PROCEDURE. The two streams decomposed the same answer independently. The lists are not aligned: the position of a claim says nothing about which claim, if any, makes the same assertion. Take each stream-2 claim in turn and read it against every stream-1 claim before deciding; then read each stream for duplicates of its own. A claim with no same-assertion partner survives unchanged.

RESTATEMENTS. A claim that restates two or more facts each stated by another claim (a summary, a FINAL ANSWER line, a sentence bundling a mechanism with its consequence) is absorbed and is never a survivor, whatever else it carries. Absorb it into the avoid or contraindication claim it restates, otherwise into the first fact it restates. It never merges the facts it restates; those stay separate survivors. A claim that applies a clause to the case (it names a case attribute: age, weeks on therapy, dose already given, a result on file) resolves one question; it is not a restatement and survives.

CITATIONS. A claim is citation-only when removing the source name leaves nothing. A content claim that names the same source absorbs it; where no content claim names that source, the citation-only claim survives.

- "avoid X" and "if X is unavoidable, <dose or monitoring>";

- a mechanism and the consequence it drives;

- a class rule and the same rule for one named drug;
- a risk-category label and the directive built on it;
- a finding and the grade an evidence table assigns to it;
