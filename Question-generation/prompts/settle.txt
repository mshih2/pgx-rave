## The settlement rule

- **A finding is acted on only if both readers made it independently.** The same span at a
  different severity or under a different label counts as agreement; findings about different
  sentences do not. Opposite positions on one span are a disagreement: record it, do not act.
- **A finding from one reader is recorded, not acted on.**
- **You may overrule a corroborated finding** when reading the material shows it is wrong. Two
  readers agreeing is not the same as being right: re-read the span, including the sentences
  before it that set its subject.
- **Do not order a repair the material cannot support.** If the material cannot settle which of
  two entries governs, the change is to name both, not to pick one. When a change deletes one of
  a redundant pair, say which of the two is the printed one to keep.
- Change the question only if both readers found a defect in it and the fix needs no material
  beyond what is above.
