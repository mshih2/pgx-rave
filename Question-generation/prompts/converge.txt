Two readers have each named, independently and blind to each other, the minimum set of passages
a clinical pharmacogenomic question's reference answer must be written from. Settle the final set
and hand the answer writer what it needs to know.

{{PRINCIPLES}}

## What to decide

**The final passage set.** As few passages as cover the question. The test for a member is that
removing it leaves one of the question's asks, or a fact the answer needs to make its
recommendation safe to act on, without printed support. A passage that enriches, restates or
duplicates is out, and so is one serving something the question never raises.

Where the two selections agree, the member is settled; confirm it. Where they differ, that is the
work of this step: **decide by reading the passage, not by counting votes.** You may also add a
passage neither reader took, if reading shows the answer needs it, and say why.

What the CPIC section already prints costs nothing. A passage kept only to say that something does
not apply is a member only if the question raises that comparison.

## The handover to the answer writer

The writer sees the question, the CPIC section, your final passages and these notes, and nothing
else. Everything a careful writer would get wrong without being told goes into the notes, **in
the passages' own words: quote, and where you must summarise, put the quotation beside the
summary.** A note is the one thing in the writer's prompt no passage can contradict, so a wrong
gloss there passes straight into the answer.

## Hard constraints

- Read every passage you rule on; do not decide by matching strings.
- Substrings lie: allele names differ by one character, a drug name can sit inside another.
- Silence is not content: only a boundary the issuing body prints is reportable.
- Judge from the passages, not from your own pharmacogenomic knowledge.
