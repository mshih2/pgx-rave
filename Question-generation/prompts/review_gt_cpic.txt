## Judge these, and only these

**1. The question.** Does it state the gene, genotype, drug, population and clinical context? Is
every detail doing a job? Is each ask answerable from the material above? Is it clinically
coherent?

**2. Coverage.** Does the answer give everything the question asks for that the material prints?
A finding names the ask and quotes the span that should have answered it.

**3. Fidelity.** Take the answer sentence by sentence and find the span that carries each claim.
- *unsupported*: no span carries it (including a number the material does not print, a converted
  unit, a value carried across a drug, phenotype, population or column);
- *distorted*: a span exists but the answer changes it (strength, hedge, condition, direction,
  scope or attribution);
- *silence*: the answer says CPIC does not address something that a span does address, or gives
  a reason for a gap that nothing prints;
- *differing entries*: two spans differ and the answer averages them or picks one without
  naming both.

**4. Concision.** A sentence or clause that could be deleted without losing a printed fact.

**5. Register.** Hedging of the writer's own, preamble, closing summary, publication identifiers,
a fact credited to the wrong kind of document.

## Rules

- Judge against the material above, not your own pharmacogenomic knowledge. A claim you believe
  is clinically wrong but the material prints is not a fidelity failure; add it as a note.
- Read the span before you call a claim supported or not; do not decide by matching strings.
  Allele names differ by one character; a drug name can sit inside another.
- Report only what you would change. A preference is not a finding.
- Every finding quotes the answer's (or the question's) words, and the material's words where it
  rests on a span.
