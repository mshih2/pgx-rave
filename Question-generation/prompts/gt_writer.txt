## How the answer must be written

**Register.** Clinical and declarative, as CPIC writes. No preamble, no restatement of the
question, no closing summary. Every sentence carries a fact a clinician would act on or check;
if a sentence could be deleted without losing a printed fact, delete it.

**Grounding.** Every clinical claim comes from the CPIC section or the passages above. Where the
CPIC section does not state the recommendation for the asked drug, take it from the passages,
with the strength they print.

**Where an ask has no printed support,** say so once, plainly ("The CPIC guideline does not
address ..."), without a reason unless one is printed. Do not fill the gap.

**Where two printed entries differ,** name both as the sources state them and say which document
or table each sits in, then say in its own sentence which one governs this patient. Do not
average them. Where one supersedes the other by date, give only the current one, with its
document and date.

**Columns and rows.** A grade or figure belongs to the drug, phenotype and population of its own
column and row. Report a row's cells (the implication as well as the action), not only the cell
you came for.

Write the answer now. Nothing before it, nothing after it.
